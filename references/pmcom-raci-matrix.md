## Definition

> A RACI matrix is a project management tool that clarifies task ownership by
> defining each team member's role in completing a task.
>
> The RACI framework — sometimes called RACI model or RACI diagram — is used for
> determining project roles to improve workflows and communication... **The
> horizontal axis represents each person on the project team and the vertical
> axis represents each task.**
>
> Each square of the matrix represents an individual, a task, and that
> individual's role within the project, either responsible, accountable,
> consulted, or informed.

## The four roles, as tabulated

| RACI role | Description | Number of assignees |
|---|---|---|
| Responsible (R) | Carries out the work required to complete a task or deliverable | One person per task |
| Accountable (A) | Owns the outcome and ensures the task meets expectations | One person per task |
| Consulted (C) | Provides input or expertise before or during the project | One or more people |
| Informed (I) | Receives updates on progress or final outcomes | One or more people |

> **Responsible.** The Responsible person is the one doing the actual work. They
> own the task from start to finish, take a hands-on approach to execution, and
> make sure the deliverable is completed on time. In most cases, a task has one
> Responsible person, though larger tasks can have more. **What stays consistent
> is that this person is expected to produce the output, not just oversee it.**
>
> **Accountable.** The Accountable person is the final check on a deliverable.
> They review the work, confirm it meets the required standard, and give the
> sign-off. **There should only be one Accountable per task.**
>
> **Consulted.** The Consulted role covers anyone whose input is needed before a
> task can progress... **Their recommendations inform decisions, but the project
> team isn't obligated to follow them.**
>
> **Informed.** The Informed group doesn't make decisions or get consulted as
> they simply need to know what's happening... Include them in kickoffs and demos
> as optional attendees, and keep them copied on major updates.

## Who builds it, and when

> The RACI matrix is created typically, but not exclusively, by the project
> manager at the start of the project. **After defining its scope and breaking
> the project down into tasks and deliverables using a work breakdown structure,
> the RACI matrix is then created** to clarify who is Responsible, Accountable,
> Consulted, or Informed for each.

## Stated limitations

Reproduced in full, because a trade-press page listing its own tool's weaknesses
is worth having on the record:

> **Limitations on role scope**: Doesn't detail what each role involves,
> particularly for verification and sign-off.
>
> **Limits on task details and scope**: Assigns ownership but doesn't specify
> what needs to be done per task.
>
> **Not ideal to the agile methodology**: RACI assigns individual responsibility;
> agile is built on shared team accountability.

## Practitioner quotes

> "RACIs are a great way to help keep your project team and stakeholders
> accountable to what they should be doing. Sometimes people forget things like
> who makes decisions or who is in charge of setting agendas, especially over the
> course of a long project." — **Molly Beran**, founder, Projects by Molly, LLC

> "[RACI matrices] force owners of the project to clear timelines for approvals,
> provide clarity, push decisions faster, and call out gaps before they become
> failures. **Many teams misplace alignment with agreement. The RACI forces
> clarity.**" — **Jamilyn Trainor**, senior project manager

> "When everyone knows what they are responsible for, multiple people don't
> unknowingly work on the same task, like writing job descriptions for the same
> position." — **Rohit Agarwal**, co-founder, Zenius

> "Assigning one Accountable person per task makes it clear who to escalate
> issues to... just clearly distinguishing two-way Consulted from one-way
> Informed in a RACI chart makes a big difference in keeping communication
> clear." — **Chandler Paul**, senior project manager, NEWMEDIA.com

Paul also reports a specific, unaudited result from introducing a RACI during a

about two-thirds and we were able to deliver the asset five business days sooner
than before."*

## The seven construction steps

> 1. **Plan ahead**: Start by communicating the project plan to key stakeholders.
> 2. **Define the scope of the project**: List down all key activities and
>    deliverables in a project document such as statement of work (SOW) or a
>    project charter.
> 3. **List the tasks**: Break each deliverable into specific tasks that require
>    clear ownership.
> 4. **Identify everyone involved**: Add team members and stakeholders who will
>    complete work, approve decisions, provide input, or receive updates.
> 5. **Create the matrix**: Place the tasks in the first column and participant
>    names across the top row.
> 6. **Assign the RACI roles**: Give every task at least one Responsible person
>    and exactly one Accountable owner. Add Consulted or Informed participants
>    only when their involvement is necessary.
> 7. **Review the chart as a group**: Hold review sessions with key members of
>    the team for alignment.

## Common mistakes

> **Starting without a project plan**: Don't build a RACI matrix before you have
> a full team, a defined scope, and a project plan.
>
> **Adding too many stakeholders**: The bigger the team, the harder the matrix is
> to manage. Too many roles and stakeholders make responsibilities harder to
> track and **accountability easier to dodge**. For large teams, break the project
> into smaller components or consider creating separate RACI matrices for
> individual workstreams or project phases.
>
> **Failing to communicate roles**: **A RACI matrix doesn't replace a
> conversation.** Before finalizing it, brief the team on their roles and the
> project's goals... and revisit the matrix regularly so no one loses track of
> their responsibilities as the project evolves.
>
> **Overburdening team members**: Avoid stacking multiple Responsible and
> Accountable roles on one person. It leads to burnout and slower output.

## Variants, per the article

| Method | Primary purpose | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| RASCI | Adds a Support role to RACI | Use when the person completing a task needs help from another contributor. |
| DACI | Assigns a Driver, Approver, Contributors, and Informed parties | Use when one person must move a decision forward while another grants final approval. |
| RAPID | Defines who Recommends, Agrees, Performs, provides Input, and Decides | Use for major decisions that require several types of authority or expertise. |

From the FAQs:

> **Is RACI outdated?** RACI is still widely used, but for more complex
> decision-making processes, alternatives like DACI and RAPID may be better
> suited. However, it remains a popular tool for establishing clear roles in
> projects, especially for smaller teams.
