### 01 — Introduction to gen AI for businesses

**What is AI?**
- Computer systems performing tasks that typically require human intelligence.
- Achieved through machine learning (ML), where systems learn from data to solve specific problems.
- Powered by an AI model, which takes an input and produces an output based on its training.

**What is generative AI?**
- A specific subset of AI that focuses on *creating* content rather than simply analyzing or responding to existing data.
- A type of AI that helps users create new content and ideas.
- A technology integrated into applications — it is not an application itself.
- Example: the Gemini app is Google's gen AI chatbot (an app); Gemini is the underlying model used to build applications (Gemini app, Workspace with Gemini, Gemini in Looker, Gemini in BigQuery, etc., and Gemini used with Google Cloud services to automate/troubleshoot).

**Four primary ways to use gen AI**
- Create: write articles/emails/social posts; create images, video, audio; generate code.
- Summarize: summarize long documents/articles; create concise reports from complex data; extract key takeaways from meetings/presentations.
- Discover: uncover hidden patterns/insights in data; search for resources/documents; monitor real-time events.
- Automate: automate format conversion; automate documentation; automate notifications/alarms.

**Multimodal generative AI**
- Enables simultaneous processing and creation of content across multiple types of data ("modalities").
- Use case pairs: text+image, text+PDF, text+video+data, sensor data+video.
- Examples: marketing team generates on-brand social posts (text+image); legal team summarizes a contract PDF and flags risks (text+PDF); market research firm analyzes sentiment across video testimonials + survey data (text+video+data); manufacturing team analyzes camera feeds + sensor data for safety hazards (sensor+video).

**Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform**
- Google Cloud's unified AI development platform — the key to creating/deploying custom gen AI solutions.
- Lets you build, train, and deploy ML models and AI applications.
- Gives access to Google's large gen AI models through Model Garden.
- Lets you tune Google's LLMs and deploy them into AI-powered applications.

### 02 — Introduction to gen AI foundations

**Traditional vs. foundation AI models**
- Traditional AI models: trained for a single purpose on specific data (e.g., filtering spam emails).
- Foundation AI models: trained on massive amounts of diverse data (text, images, code), adapting to many tasks. Examples: LLMs, Nano Banana, Chirp.

**What foundation models do**
- Develop a deep understanding of the data.
- Adapt to a wide range of downstream tasks.
- Streamline processes.
- Automate tasks.
- Unlock new possibilities across business functions.

**Key features of foundation models**
- Trained on diverse data: wide variety of data → general patterns/relationships applicable to different tasks.
- Flexible: one model supports a wide range of use cases.
- Adaptable: can be specialized for particular domains via additional targeted training.

**Examples of foundation models**
- Gemini: trained on a massive multimodal dataset (text, images, code, audio, video); performs tasks across domains.
- Nano Banana: trained on images + text descriptions; generates, edits, and understands images.
- Chirp: trained on large multilingual audio dataset; designed for speech recognition.

**Prompting**
- All AI models take inputs/prompts; foundation models (especially multimodal ones) are far more flexible in what they accept.
- Prompting is one of the most important skills for maximizing value from gen AI models/tools.
- Prompt → output examples: a question → the answer; request for an image from text → the image; request to summarize a file → the summary; code with errors → corrected code.

**Key takeaways**
- Foundation models are large-scale, general-purpose models trained on massive data.
- They adapt to a wide range of downstream tasks.
- They're accessed through prompting — a skill developed through practice and experimentation.

### 03 — Gen AI strategy

**Google innovative AI products timeline**: 2001 ML for spell check → 2006 Google Translate → 2015 Google Search (deep learning) → 2016 TPUs → 2018 Smart Compose in Gmail → 2023 Bard → 2023 Gemini.

**Google research advancements timeline**: 2008 Google Cloud → 2012 deep learning in speech recognition → 2014 DeepMind acquisition → 2015 TensorFlow → 2017 Transformer → 2018 BERT / AI Principles → 2024 Gemma.

**Leverage Google's gen AI ecosystem — five pillars**
- Individual productivity and efficiency: Google Search (faster/accurate info); Workspace with Gemini (draft emails in Gmail, generate Slides, summarize in Docs, automate Sheets); Gemini App; Gemini for Google Cloud (build apps/services).
- Continuous improvement: automatic model upgrades; early access to new Gemini features; security patches/updates.
- Responsible AI: Secure AI Framework (SAIF) — tools/best practices for secure AI systems; Mandiant — threat intelligence to protect AI systems; AI Principles — Google's published principles guiding AI development/deployment; Responsible AI toolkit — build fair/unbiased/socially-beneficial AI.
- Enterprise ready: Agent Platform — unified platform for building/deploying ML models with enterprise-grade security, scalability, compliance; Google Cloud's security infrastructure (encryption, access control, network security); compliance certifications across industry standards.
- Open approach: contributions to TensorFlow, PyTorch, JAX; open-source models and datasets released to the research community; support for open standards for AI interoperability/data exchange.

**Embracing the potential of gen AI**
- Most successful gen AI implementations prioritize a clear vision and focus on impactful use cases.
- Tracking results is key to successfully implementing gen AI and achieving competitive advantage.

**Building a successful AI strategy — multi-directional (top-down + bottom-up)**
- Top-down: create a clear, compelling AI vision aligned with strategic business priorities; identify the most important use cases/workflows; then consider how AI supports those priorities.
- Bottom-up: encourage teams to share challenges/ideas for gen AI solutions; encourage small-scale experimentation, then share solutions for broader implementation.
- Executives/high-level management: executive sponsorship + clear vision is essential; ensure initiatives align with business goals, receive adequate resources, gain org-wide support, and empower experimentation.
- Mid-level managers/ICs: proximity to daily operations/end-users gives invaluable insight; champion adoption by identifying high-impact feasible solutions and encouraging experimentation/feedback.

**Six strategy factors (exec/high-level view vs. mid-level/IC view)**
- Strategic focus — Exec: focus on a high-impact area then expand; prioritize use cases that are feasible, actionable, affordable, and high ROI. IC: identify pressing pain points; understand user needs/workflows; explore low-risk use cases.
- Exploration — Exec: empower employees to experiment; foster a collaborative environment for sharing findings. IC: experiment with different gen AI tools/apps; share findings with team/colleagues.
- Responsible AI — Exec: establish ethical guidelines and safety mechanisms; institute data governance; implement content moderation policy; monitor performance/impact. IC: adhere to company AI standards; think proactively about pitfalls; conduct testing for bias/safety/ethics.
- Resourcing — Exec: create a robust data strategy; leverage existing tools/platforms; invest in AI talent. IC: leverage resources available within the team; make the case for additional resources by showing value/ROI.
- Impact — Exec: set clear goals and KPIs; communicate progress/impact to stakeholders. IC: demonstrate tangible impact of experiments to gain support; articulate how initiatives contribute to business goals; define/track KPIs.
- Continuous improvement — Exec: embrace iterative development; establish a regular evaluation cadence; implement feedback mechanisms. IC: perform continuous testing/measurement/refinement from user feedback; update solutions regularly; continue gen AI education.

**Augmentation vs. automation**
- Gen AI augments (assists, doesn't replace) human: critical thinking & problem solving, creativity & innovation, relationship building & collaboration, strategic planning & vision. Gen AI provides data/insights/ideas/forecasts, but humans interpret, decide, build trust, and set long-term direction.
- Gen AI automates tasks that are: repetitive and rule-based (data entry, information retrieval, content formatting, basic code generation) or time-consuming and resource-intensive (research, data analysis, content summarization, initial draft creation).
- Human-in-the-loop stages: data selection & preparation (ensure training data is high-quality/representative) → prompt design & refinement (craft prompts for accurate/useful responses) → output evaluation & refinement (review/edit generated content for accuracy, relevance, brand alignment) → continuous monitoring & feedback (feedback on performance, identify improvement areas).

**Augmentation/automation practice scenarios**
1. Gen AI chatbot handling basic pricing/availability questions → **Automation** (repetitive task, frees sales team for complex interactions).
2. Financial analyst uses gen AI to analyze data/generate reports, then advises clients → **Augmentation** (AI enhances the analyst's role; human still interprets and advises).
3. Online retailer auto-generates product descriptions from attributes → **Automation** (labor-intensive task automated at scale).
4. Dev team uses gen AI to generate standard code, then reviews/integrates it → **Augmentation** (increases efficiency; human oversight remains essential).
